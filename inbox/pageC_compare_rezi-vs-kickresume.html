Rezi vs Kickresume: same test, different failures

By CareerRadar Lab · Last verified Jun 2026 · Batches GH-2026Q2-rezi GH-2026Q2-kick
The 20-second answer
Pick Rezi if…
…you're applying to ATS-heavy mid/large companies and want keyword targeting against a job description. It won our parse test on both platforms and the $149 lifetime deal beats subscriptions for long searches.
Pick Kickresume if…
…your applications also go to humans first — startups, agencies, design-adjacent roles. Its templates look meaningfully better, and its single-column ones parsed nearly as well as Rezi's.
Pick neither if…
…you already have a solid resume and just worry about ATS readability. A plain single-column document parsed 18/18 in our baseline runs — run the free 2-minute parse check before paying anyone.

Head-to-head: verified parse results

Both tools generated the same fictional candidate's resume (three most-popular templates each), exported to PDF, uploaded to live Greenhouse and Lever forms — 3 runs per template, no submissions. Same protocol as our ATS Parse Study.

Kickresume's skills failures all came from its two “creative” templates, which render skills as rated bars — a graphical element parsers drop, consistent with our icon-list finding. Its single-column “Simple” template passed every field.

Machine readability — winner: Rezi

Rezi's defaults are conservative by design and it shows in the data: 60/72 clean passes vs Kickresume's 61/72 only if you avoid its creative templates. Choose Kickresume's wrong template and you silently lose your skills section.

Design quality for human readers — winner: Kickresume

Not everything is a parser. For roles where a person opens your PDF first, Kickresume's typography and layout options are a clear step up — Rezi's exports are functional but plain.

AI writing quality — tie (both generic)

Honest note: both tools' AI bullets read templated for specialized roles. Neither replaces rewriting your own experience. We score parse performance because we can measure it; we won't pretend to score prose.

Pricing for a long search — winner: Rezi

Rezi's $149 lifetime beats Kickresume's subscription past month three. For a short sprint, both monthly plans cost about the same — watch both tools' auto-renewal defaults.

FAQ

Which one is better for beating ATS screening?

Neither "beats" anything — that framing sells fear. What we can verify: Rezi's default templates produced more complete field extraction in our June 2026 batch. Extraction affects whether recruiters can find and filter you; it doesn't decide interviews.

Do commissions affect this comparison?

Both tools pay similar referral commissions, so we earn roughly the same either way — and Teal, which pays us nothing, still appears in our category rankings above both for budget users. The edge calls in the table come from run-level records you can open yourself.

Will these results change?

Yes — parsers and templates both update. We re-run this batch quarterly; the changelog on our study page records every change, including when a tool improves.
